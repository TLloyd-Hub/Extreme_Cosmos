Quasars

A quasar (short for "quasi-stellar radio source") is an incredibly luminous and distant active galactic nucleus. Powered by supermassive black holes feeding on massive amounts of gas and dust, they can outshine entire galaxies by factors of thousands.
    How Quasars Work:

  • The Engine: At the center of a quasar is a supermassive black hole with a mass of millions to billions of suns.

  • Accretion Disk: As matter is drawn toward the black hole, it forms a rapidly spinning disk. Extreme gravitational and frictional forces heat this gas to millions of degrees, causing it to shine intensely.

  • Plasma Jets: Some of the highly accelerated matter escapes along the black hole's magnetic field lines, shooting outward into space at nearly the speed of light.
