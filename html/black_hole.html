Black Hole

A black hole is one of the most mysterious objects in our universe. It is a region of space where the gravitational forces are so strong that nothing can escape, not even light. Black holes form when a massive star goes supernova at the end of its life cycle, collapsing into a infinitely dense point called a singularity.

Every black hole has an area around it called an event horizon. The event horizon near the black hole is a boundary where, once inside, matter and light can only move towards the black hole. Nothing can escape from inside the event horizon.

    The Four Main Types:

  • Stellar-mass: Formed from the collapse of a massive star; typically 5 to 100 times the mass of the Sun.

  • Supermassive: Millions to billions of times the mass of the Sun. These sit at the centers of almost all large galaxies, including our own Milky Way (known as Sagittarius A*).

  • Intermediate-mass: Ranging from hundreds to hundreds of thousands of solar masses.

  • Primordial: Tiny black holes that may have formed in the early universe, potentially the size of a single atom.
