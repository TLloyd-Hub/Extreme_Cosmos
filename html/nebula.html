Nebulae

A nebula is a giant cloud of dust and gas in space. Some nebulae are formed from the gas and dust thrown out by an exploding star when it goes supernova. They can create beautiful shapes in space and often have names based on the structure that they resemble.

Nebulae are star nurseries where new stars are born. The dense clouds of gas and dust clump together over millions of years until they become big and hot enough to ignite nuclear fusion into a new star.

Types of Nebulae:

Monkey Head Nebula

Emission Nebula:

A nebula that is formed of high-energy ionized gases that cause them to radiate various wavelengths of light. They are energized by the ultraviolet radiation emitted by a nearby hot star.

Witch Head Nebula

Reflection Nebula:

This type of nebula reflects the light of nearby stars. Since it is not close enough to the stars to be ionized and form an emission nebula, it appears as the color of the stars that are supplying the light, often a blue color.

Horse Head Nebula

Dark Nebula:

The cloud of a dark nebula is so dense that it blocks the light from the objects behind it. They are only visible using radio waves or on the infrared spectrum.

Red Spider Nebula

Planetary Nebula:

This type of nebula is created by the expanding remains of a star similar to the size of our Sun. The strong stellar winds expel the outer layers forming a gaseous shell with the white dwarf core of the star left in the middle.

Jellyfish Nebula

Supernova Remnants:

These nebulae are formed from the expanding remains of a supernova explosion. They often glow brightly due to the ionization of the gas in the shell of the remaining gas during the supernova event.
