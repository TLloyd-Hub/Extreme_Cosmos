Nebula

A nebula is a giant cloud of dust and gas in space. Some nebulae (more than one nebula) come from the gas and dust thrown out by the explosion of a dying star, such as a supernova. Other nebulae are regions where new stars are beginning to form.
    Key Types of Nebulae:

  • Emission Nebulae: Clouds of high-energy gas that are energized by ultraviolet radiation from nearby stars, causing them to glow vibrantly (e.g., the Orion Nebula).

  • Reflection Nebulae: Clouds of dust that do not emit their own light but reflect the light of nearby, bright stars, often appearing blue.

  • Dark Nebulae: Dense clouds of dust and gas that are so thick they block out the light from stars or other objects behind them (e.g., the Horsehead Nebula).

  • Planetary Nebulae: The expanding, glowing shells of gas cast off by dying stars that are similar in mass to our Sun.

  • Supernova Remnants: The shattered, expanding debris left behind after a massive star explodes at the end of its life (e.g. the Crab Nebula).
