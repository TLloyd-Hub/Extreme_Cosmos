Supernovae

A supernova is the colossal explosion of a star. These cataclysmic events are so energetic that they can briefly outshine an entire galaxy, radiating as much energy in a few days as our Sun does in billions of years. They forge heavy elements like iron, gold, and silver, dispersing them across the universe to seed future generations of stars and planets
    There are two primary ways a star can trigger this massive explosion:

  • Core-Collapse (Type II): This occurs when a massive star (at least 8 times heavier than our Sun) runs out of nuclear fuel. Without outward pressure to combat gravity, the core collapses in a fraction of a second, resulting in a violent rebound explosion. The remnant core left behind becomes either a dense neutron star or a black hole.

  • Thermonuclear (Type Ia): This happens in a binary star system where a small, dead star—a white dwarf—pulls too much material from a companion star. Once the white dwarf accumulates too much mass, runaway nuclear fusion is triggered, obliterating the star entirely.
